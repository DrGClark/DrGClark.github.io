I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science in the [School of Computing](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/soc/) at the [University of South Alabama](https://www.southalabama.edu/), where I conduct research at the intersection of cybersecurity and robotics. My research focuses on protecting autonomous systems from cyber threats, with particular emphasis on hardware security, side-channel attacks, and cyber-physical systems security.

Before joining USA in August 2022, I was an Assistant Professor of Computer Information Systems at Spring Hill College (2018–2022), and prior to that served as an Adjunct Instructor in Computer Science at USA (2017–2018). I spent over 15 years in industry in software and control systems engineering roles at Precision Engineering Inc., Mentor Graphics Corporation, Sterling Commerce, Accelerated Technology, and Omniphone Inc.

I earned my Ph.D. in Computing (2019), M.S. in Computer Science (1999), and B.S.E.E. in Electrical Engineering (1992), all from the University of South Alabama. I am a licensed Professional Engineer in Alabama (inactive), a Senior Member of IEEE, and a member of the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM).

My current research interests include:
- Cybersecurity in robotic and autonomous systems
- Hardware security and side-channel analysis (power, EM, acoustic)
- Adversarial machine learning and defense for autonomous platforms
- Cyber-physical systems security and intrusion detection
- FPGA security, hardware trojan detection, and trusted electronics
- Edge AI security for self-driving vehicles

I am actively involved in several funded research projects, including the NSF CyberCorps® Scholarship for Service program, Department of Defense (DoD) contracts (Project Orion), and university grants on side-channel analysis and robotic anomaly detection. I currently have an NSF CAREER proposal under review. I serve as Faculty Advisor for the USA Robotics Club and the Society of Automotive Engineers student chapter.

I welcome inquiries from prospective graduate students interested in these research areas.
