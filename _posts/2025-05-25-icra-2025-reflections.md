Last week, I had the opportunity to attend the IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA) 2025 in Atlanta, Georgia. ICRA is one of the premier conferences in robotics research, bringing together researchers, engineers, and practitioners from around the world.

## Conference Highlights

### Keynote Presentations

The keynote talks covered fascinating ground, from bio-inspired robotics to the latest advances in reinforcement learning for manipulation tasks. Particularly relevant to our work was a presentation on adversarial robustness in vision systems—a topic closely aligned with our research on security vulnerabilities in autonomous systems.

### Research Trends

Several clear trends emerged from the technical sessions:

**1. End-to-End Learning**
Many researchers are moving toward end-to-end learning systems where neural networks directly map sensor inputs to control outputs, bypassing traditional perception-planning-control pipelines. While this approach shows promise, it also raises significant security concerns that our lab is investigating.

**2. Sim-to-Real Transfer**
There's increasing focus on bridging the "reality gap"—training robots in simulation and transferring learned behaviors to physical systems. This aligns well with our digital twin research for robot path planning.

**3. Human-Robot Collaboration**
Safe and effective human-robot interaction continues to be a major theme, with new work on predicting human intentions and ensuring safety in shared workspaces.

**4. Robust Perception**
Given real-world deployment challenges, there's growing interest in perception systems that work reliably in varying conditions—crucial for both performance and security.

## Security Considerations

What struck me most was how little explicit attention was paid to security concerns in many presentations. As roboticists push the boundaries of what autonomous systems can do, we must ensure these systems are resilient against adversarial attacks and cyber threats.

Our research operates at this important intersection—not just making robots more capable, but ensuring they're secure and trustworthy.

## Networking and Collaboration

Conferences are invaluable for building connections. I had productive discussions with researchers working on:

- Adversarial attacks on object detection systems
- Formal verification for autonomous systems
- Privacy-preserving robot perception
- Secure multi-robot coordination

These conversations may lead to future collaborations and inform our ongoing work.

## Takeaways for Our Lab

Several ideas emerged that we'll explore in our research:

1. **Benchmarking Security**: Develop standardized benchmarks for evaluating robustness of robotic systems against adversarial attacks
2. **Privacy in Perception**: Investigate methods for robots to perform tasks while respecting privacy concerns
3. **Explainable Security**: Make our security analysis tools more interpretable for robotics practitioners

## Looking Ahead

The robotics field continues to advance rapidly, presenting both exciting opportunities and important challenges. As automation becomes more prevalent, ensuring these systems are secure, safe, and trustworthy becomes increasingly critical.

I'm grateful for the opportunity to participate in ICRA 2025 and look forward to contributing to these important discussions through our research.
